This is a televised political debate (BBC Question Time), annotated the way the expert annotators of the QT30 corpus annotate it (Inference Anchoring Theory).

The dialogue lists turns T1, T2, ... in the order they were spoken, as "Speaker: words". Under each turn are the propositions it expresses, P1, P2, ...: each restates what was said so that it stands on its own (pronouns resolved; a question may use "xxx" for its unknown part). Propositions that couldn't be matched to a turn are listed at the end.

Relations between two propositions:
- Inference: one proposition is a reason, evidence or explanation for the other, and it is a different point from the one it supports. The reason may come before or after the point it supports ("X because Y" and "Y, so X" both mean Y supports X).
- Rephrase: the later proposition makes the same point as the earlier one again: it restates, reformulates or narrows it, or gives a sharper or more concrete version. An answer rephrases the question it answers. If the later proposition mainly repeats or elaborates the earlier point rather than giving a separate reason for it, it is Rephrase, not Inference.
- Conflict: the later proposition attacks the earlier one: it contradicts, rebuts or undermines it, or the two cannot both be held. This includes a speaker attacking their own earlier point, as in a concession or contrast ("it is right to plan for the future, but we must not lose sight of the present"). Most annotated Conflicts are within one speaker's talk, and most have no "but" or "however".
- None: no direct relation. Two propositions on the same topic are often not directly related.

How the annotators work (measured on 1,250 annotated maps):
- Relations mostly join neighbouring turns: 69% are one turn apart and 84% at most two.
- 94% of Inferences join propositions by the same speaker. Rephrases are mostly same-speaker too, apart from answers to questions.
- Conflict is rare: most maps have none.
- Annotators link sparingly. About half of all propositions start no relation, almost all the rest start exactly one, and only 1 in 20 starts two or more. Each proposition is linked to the one proposition it most directly bears on, usually nearby.

How a turn puts its proposition forward:
- Asserting: states it as true (about 90% of turns).
- Pure Questioning: a genuine question, with no commitment to an answer.
- Assertive Questioning: question form, but the speaker also conveys their own position, or steers the discussion.
- Rhetorical Questioning: a question asked to make a point, not to get an answer.
- Agreeing: agrees with an earlier claim ("yes", "exactly"); the proposition is the claim agreed with.
- Challenging: challenges an earlier claim and demands a justification.
- Default Illocuting: rare; only when the turn plainly fits none of the others.
